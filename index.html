Sacred Art of South India

The Grace of Bharatanatyam

Bharatanatyam is one of India's oldest and most revered classical dance forms. Originating in the temples of Tamil Nadu, this sacred art synthesizes rhythm (Bhava), emotion (Raga), and rhythm (Tala)—culminating in an extraordinary visual hymn of poise, dramatic power, and architectural beauty.

Pure Dance
Dimension One

Nritta

Pure dance and rhythmic movement

Nritta represents abstract dance composed of rhythmic foot patterns, hand gestures (mudras), and sculptural bodily postures without specific narrative meaning. It highlights technical precision, speed, and geometric symmetry.

  • Adavus & Jathis synchronization
  • Araimandi (half-sitting posture)
  • Chari & Mandala foot dynamics
Expression
Dimension Two

Nritya

Expression and storytelling through movement

Nritya weaves rhythm together with emotional expression (Bhava) and narrative depiction (Abhinaya). The dancer uses facial mime, eyes, and mudras to convey poetry, devotional verses, and spiritual love.

  • Navarasa (nine fundamental emotions)
  • Vachika & Angika Abhinaya
  • Padams, Javalis & Varnams
Drama
Dimension Three

Natya

Dramatic representation and storytelling

Natya is the theatrical element encompassing character portrayals, dramatic dialogues, and grand mythological productions where dancers embody deities, mythological heroes, and sacred tales.

  • Full dance dramas & ballets
  • Epics: Ramayana, Mahabharata & Bhagavatam
  • Traditional Aharya (costume & decorum)
The Language of Hands

Hastamudras: 28 Single-Hand Gestures

In Bharatanatyam, the hands speak when words fall silent. From the sacred Pataka flag to the open lotus of Alapadma, every mudra carries centuries of spiritual connotation and narrative precision.

Tripataka Crown / Tree
Alapadma Full Lotus
